A fair question, asked on most first calls: with everything ever homologated, why run the
Ford Escort?

## It was right the first time

The Escort's rally record isn't nostalgia — it's data. Light, rear-driven, balanced, and
simple in the way that matters: when something breaks four hundred kilometres from the
workshop, simplicity is a spare part you carry for free.

## The modern build removes the wrong half of "old"

A properly built Safari-spec car keeps the geometry and the character and replaces
everything that used to make old cars frightening: the shell is prepared to modern
standards, the cage and seats and extinguisher systems are current, the cooling is designed
for the equator rather than for Wales.

What's left is the honest part — a car that tells you everything the road is doing, at
speeds where you can actually listen.

## It's the right amount of fast

Modern machinery covers ground faster than most first-time crews can read it. The Escort's
pace is the pace at which East Africa is still legible: quick enough that the finish means
something, humane enough that you'll remember the week rather than just surviving it.

## And it sounds correct

We could dress this up as engineering. It's also just true: a twin-cam Escort at full noise
on murram is one of the great sounds of the sport, and you're allowed to want it.
