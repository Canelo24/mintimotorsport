The roadbook is the quietest piece of technology in rallying. Paper, ink, and a convention
old enough to have grandchildren. It is also the reason two people in a loud car can cross
a subcontinent without an argument. Mostly.

## The tulip

Each instruction is a small diagram called a tulip, with a ball for where you come from and
an arrow for where you are going. Between tulips, a distance. That is the whole language,
and like all good notation it holds exactly as much as you can process at speed.

## Distance is the co-driver's heartbeat

The trip meter is the co-driver's instrument, and the rhythm of a good crew is calls made
by distance and confirmed by landscape. Eight hundred. Four hundred. Junction, right, onto
the bigger road. When the numbers and the world agree, the car flows.

## Trust is the actual technology

A driver flat over a crest is acting on a sentence spoken by someone reading a page. That
is the sport reduced to its essence: preparation converted into trust, at speed. Everything
in a programme, from the shakedown week to the co-driver pairing, is really the manufacture
of that trust before it gets tested.

## Why our site looks the way it does

Regulars will have noticed the column down the left edge of these pages behaves like a
roadbook. Tulips, distances, a finish control. That is not decoration. It is the grammar
this team thinks in, applied to the one stage we run on the internet.
