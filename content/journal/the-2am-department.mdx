Every arrive-and-drive brochure photographs the jump. Nobody photographs 2am, which is a
shame, because 2am is what you're actually buying.

Here is what happens after the driver goes to bed.

## The car comes apart

Not dramatically. Methodically. Wheels off, guards off, the underside inspected by torch and
by thumb. Murram is honest but relentless — it doesn't break cars with one hit, it loosens
them a thousand small ways. The crew's job is to find every one of those thousand before
tomorrow's first stage does.

## The time card sets the tempo

Service isn't finished when the car is finished; it's finished when the schedule says so.
A good crew chief runs the halt backwards from the out-time: what must happen, what should
happen, what can wait for the next halt without becoming tomorrow's crisis. Watching one work
is like watching a conductor who happens to hold a torque wrench.

## Spares are a philosophy

You cannot carry everything, so what you carry is a theory about what the road will do.
Which corners of that theory get proven wrong — and how fast the crew adapts when they are —
is most of the difference between teams that finish and teams that explain.

## Why this page exists

Because when we say a seat includes "full service crew", this is the sentence behind the
sentence. The driver's week is the visible tenth of the programme. The other nine tenths
happen under floodlights, in the smell of brake cleaner and milky tea, done by people who
would honestly rather be nowhere else.
